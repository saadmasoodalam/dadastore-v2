## 6. Topic-specific replacements

### Creative Testing

The revised article now gives direct guidance on:

- defining the budget or production decision a test must support;
- separating hooks, angles, formats, proof approaches, and offers;
- holding audience, placement, optimization event, destination, and other variables stable where practical;
- creating genuinely comparable controls;
- labeling assets, concepts, landing-page versions, audiences, and placements;
- writing hypotheses as causal questions rather than forecasts;
- naming evidence that would contradict a creative premise;
- designing testing-board cells around questions instead of asset volume;
- checking spend, impressions, reach, frequency, placement mix, and delivery balance before judging creative;
- separating ad evidence from landing-page or offer changes;
- treating thin samples as directional or inconclusive;
- producing reusable learning statements and next-brief instructions;
- validating claims, accessibility, crops, captions, sound-off meaning, and source approvals; and
- closing or extending tests through creative-specific criteria.

The article continues to warn against false conclusions from unstable controls, simultaneous changes, unequal delivery, and unsupported winner claims.

### Technical SEO

The revised article now gives direct guidance on:

- scoping templates, markets, subdomains, and important page groups;
- sampling healthy, redirected, canonicalized, paginated, filtered, localized, and error-state URLs;
- recording crawler configuration, rendering mode, authentication, log availability, and release context;
- comparing HTTP status, robots directives, canonical targets, sitemap membership, rendered content, and observed indexation state;
- distinguishing correct `404`/`410` behavior from unintended exclusion;
- classifying findings as blocks, conflicts, degradations, or observations;
- prioritizing by affected templates, page importance, customer impact, reversibility, and release risk;
- verifying navigation, breadcrumbs, pagination, crawlable links, script-dependent discovery, redirects, chains, and depth;
- handling parameters, variants, duplicates, canonicals, and broad crawl-control rules cautiously;
- validating structured data against visible page content and supported schema requirements;
- testing rendered mobile content and realistic page-performance conditions;
- separating laboratory scores from customer and crawl evidence;
- building implementation-ready backlog entries without invented traffic, ranking, or revenue estimates; and
- retesting the same representative URL set after release while preserving residual risk.

The article maintains its explicit boundary that large rendering, migration, international, canonical, robots, navigation, or architecture changes may require qualified SEO and engineering review.
