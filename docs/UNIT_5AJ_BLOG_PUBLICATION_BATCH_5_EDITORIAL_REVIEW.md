## 4. Batch-selection method

The full 25-draft registry was compared against the 36 published articles and the current editorial rules. Selection favored:

1. clear standalone intent and a practical audience problem;
2. low topic overlap with the published library;
3. working metadata, word count, local links, mobile toggle, and one topic-specific visual module;
4. existing public internal-link opportunities;
5. category gaps; and
6. the smallest defensible revision scope.

Seven deferred drafts from Batches 1–4 contained no exact shared template sentences across the remaining draft pool and were stronger than most later drafts. Three additional articles were selected to open Content Marketing, Email Marketing, and Marketing Automation coverage. Those three are strategically useful, but evidence shows repeated batch-template prose and substantive sections placed after the CTA; they require major revision rather than publication.

The other Batch 5 and Batch 6 drafts were not automatically selected merely because of their generation batch. Each contains 12 exact sentences reused across its respective batch family and therefore conflicts with the instruction to avoid repeated template prose.
