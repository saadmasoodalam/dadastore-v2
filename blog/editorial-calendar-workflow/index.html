How to Build an Editorial Calendar Around Business Priorities

Coordinate themes, customer questions, formats, owners, evidence, approvals, distribution, updates, and measurement in a workable calendar.

By DaDaStoreEditorial draft9 min read

An editorial calendar is not a list of publication dates. It is a shared operating view of customer questions, business priorities, evidence, formats, owners, dependencies, approvals, distribution, and maintenance. When those decisions live elsewhere, the calendar looks full while production remains unpredictable and strategic gaps stay hidden.

Build the Editorial Workflow

Add a monthly calendar retrospective

Compare planned work, completed work, delayed work, and unplanned requests. For each change, record whether the cause was capacity, evidence, approval, dependency, distribution, or a deliberate priority decision. Avoid judging the team only by publication count; a stopped weak asset can be a better outcome than filling an assigned date.

Use the retrospective to adjust active work limits, briefing lead time, review ownership, and maintenance capacity. Look for themes that received repeated output without a clear customer response and important questions that never reached production. Carry only explicit actions into the next planning window. The calendar improves when it becomes a record of choices and constraints, not when every empty cell is filled.

Share a simplified view with stakeholders who need visibility but do not manage production. Show the theme, customer question, owner, stage, intended date, and current blocker without exposing unnecessary working detail. Keep the operational view for the people responsible for briefs, evidence, reviews, and distribution. Different views should draw from the same record so updates do not diverge. This reduces status meetings while preserving one accountable source for the decisions that shape the editorial program.
