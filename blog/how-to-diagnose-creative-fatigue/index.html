Separate audience saturation, delivery changes, offer weakness, journey friction, tracking problems, and genuine creative wear-out.

Create an evidence-based fatigue record

Keep one dated record for the symptom, competing explanations, supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, action, and review date. Include screenshots or exports with their settings, plus releases or business events that may affect interpretation. Do not overwrite the original diagnosis after the campaign changes; append the result so the team can see whether its reasoning held.

Over time, compare records to learn which patterns are genuinely useful in this account and which repeatedly send the team toward the wrong fix. The objective is not a universal fatigue threshold. It is a faster, more disciplined way to distinguish wear-out from delivery, offer, journey, or measurement problems while preserving enough context to reverse an unsuccessful intervention.

Set a threshold for how much diagnosis is proportionate to the decision. A small routine refresh may need a concise review, while a major budget or positioning change deserves broader evidence and stakeholder input. Avoid delaying obvious technical repairs while debating creative interpretation. Likewise, avoid launching a costly replacement program when the evidence only supports a narrow format or audience adjustment. The diagnostic process should reduce decision risk, not become an elaborate ritual that prevents timely, reversible action.
